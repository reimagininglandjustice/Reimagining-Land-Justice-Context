Lillie A. Estes (1959-2019)

Community Strategist. Organizer. Queen Mother.

Lillie Anne Estes was a visionary community strategist and organizer whose work centered the dignity, power, and self-determination of public housing residents in Richmond, Virginia. A longtime resident of Gilpin Court, Estes became a leading voice for housing justice, anti-poverty advocacy, and resident-led community development, insisting that communities most impacted by disinvestment should shape the decisions affecting their lives.

Estes served on the City of Richmond’s anti-poverty commission and worked across education, community justice, and grassroots organizing spaces, including roles with the Community Justice Network and Richmond Public Schools. Known for her deeply relational approach to organizing, she mobilized neighbors around issues of housing, public safety, education, and economic opportunity.

Estes spearheaded the creation of the Charles S. Gilpin Community Farm, a public green space and one of the growing sites within Richmond’s Food Justice Corridor. Built from her vision for collective care and neighborhood empowerment, the garden became a place where residents could grow food, gather, learn, and build community together.

Remembered as both a fierce advocate and a compassionate leader, Estes organized with humanity and grace. Her work earned recognition from organizations across Virginia and beyond, including Mothers for Justice and Equality, which honored her as “A Mother of Courage.” Her legacy continues through the communities, relationships, and spaces she helped cultivate.
