George Jackson (1941-1971)

A revolutionary thinker, author, and political prisoner, George Jackson became a symbol of Black resistance against systemic racism, mass incarceration, and economic exploitation. While imprisoned in California, he wrote powerful works like Soledad Brother, exposing how prisons functioned as tools of racial and class control.

Jackson saw land, labor, and freedom as deeply interconnected, arguing that the oppression of Black people in America was rooted in stolen land and stolen labor. He linked the struggles of incarcerated people to broader fights for liberation, viewing prisons as extensions of colonial violence.

His writings continue to influence movements for prison abolition, land justice, and radical transformation of oppressive systems.
