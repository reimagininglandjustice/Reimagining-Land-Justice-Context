Fannie Lou Hamer (1917-1977)

A fearless civil rights leader and community organizer, Fannie Lou Hamer fought for Black political power, voting rights, and economic independence in the rural South. Born to sharecroppers in Mississippi, she understood firsthand how landlessness and poverty were used to oppress Black communities.

She co-founded the Freedom Farm Cooperative in 1969, a groundbreaking project that provided Black families with land, food, and resources to build self-reliance outside the white-controlled system. Hamer believed that owning land was key to true freedom, famously saying, “If you give a man a piece of land, he can feed himself.”

Her legacy lives on in land justice movements focused on empowerment through collective ownership and food sovereignty.
