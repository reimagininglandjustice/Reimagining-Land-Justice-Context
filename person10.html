Martin Luther King Jr. (1929-1968)

A leading figure in the American Civil Rights Movement, Martin Luther King Jr. advocated for racial equality, economic justice, and nonviolent resistance to oppression. While best known for his fight against segregation and for voting rights, King also recognized the importance of economic empowerment and land access for Black Americans.

In his later years, King broadened his focus to include poverty and the need for economic redistribution. He spoke out about the systemic inequality that kept Black communities landless and economically marginalized, particularly in the South. He advocated for policies that would provide better housing, jobs, and access to resources for Black people, viewing land and economic justice as vital components of true freedom.

His vision of a "Beloved Community" emphasized not only racial equality but also equitable access to land, resources, and opportunities for all people.
