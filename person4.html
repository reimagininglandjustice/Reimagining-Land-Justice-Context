Edna Lewis (1916-2006)

A celebrated chef, writer, and advocate for Southern food traditions, Edna Lewis used cooking as a way to honor Black agricultural heritage and rural self-sufficiency. Raised in a farming community in Virginia founded by formerly enslaved people, she drew deep connections between land, food, and cultural memory.

Through her cookbooks and public work, she preserved and uplifted African American culinary traditions rooted in the land—highlighting seasonal, locally grown ingredients and sustainable practices. Her philosophy centered on respecting the earth, valuing community, and reclaiming food as a source of pride and resistance.

Her legacy continues to inspire food justice movements that tie land access, cultural identity, and nourishment together.
