Happily Natural

Rooted in Resistance. Growing in Power.

Founded in 2003 by Duron Chavis at the Black History Museum & Cultural Center of Virginia, Happily Natural Day began as a grassroots event celebrating natural hair, holistic health, and Black consciousness. What started in a museum parking lot quickly grew into a major cultural movement.

The festival fused education and culture — pairing lectures on wellness and liberation with hip hop, spoken word, and African drum circles. Over the years, it expanded into Black Freedom Weekend, a multi-day celebration drawing hundreds from across the region.

appily Natural Day has featured influential voices like Queen Afua, Dr. Llaila Afrika, and dead prez, and built strong partnerships with organizations like Camp Diva, UNIA-ACL, and VCU’s NAACP. It also launched initiatives like the Tell the Truth about Jamestown campaign and the Open Your Third Eye Project.

In May 2020, Happily Natural officially gained nonprofit 501(c)(3) status, solidifying its role as a lasting institution in Black cultural and land justice organizing.

Since then, Happily Natural has evolved beyond the festival to become a central hub for Central Virginia’s urban and community farming movements, supporting initiatives like Sankofa Community Orchard and the Central Virginia Urban Farmer Fellowship.

Today, Happily Natural Day continues to plant seeds of freedom — cultivating wellness, creativity, and liberation in Richmond and beyond.
